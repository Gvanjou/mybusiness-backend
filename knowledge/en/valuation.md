## How small businesses are valued
Small businesses are usually valued on the profit they produce for the owner, not on revenue. A common measure is owner's earnings (SDE): net profit plus the owner's own salary and one-off costs. Small, owner-run businesses often sell for roughly 1–3 times annual owner's earnings; more stable, less owner-dependent businesses can justify more. Equipment value sets a floor; a business that only breaks even is worth roughly its equipment, transferable lease and brand.

## Payback period
Payback = price ÷ annual net profit to the owner. A business for 60,000 GEL earning 2,000 GEL net per month (24,000 GEL a year) pays back in 2.5 years, before taxes and before paying yourself if you work in it. If you would have to work full-time in the business, subtract a fair salary for your own time before calculating payback.

## ROI
Return on investment (ROI) = annual net profit ÷ total investment × 100%. Total investment includes the price, renovation, initial stock, working capital for the first months, and transaction costs (lawyer, due diligence, notary).

## Is the asking price fair?
Compare the asking price with: (1) verified profit and the multiples above, (2) the value of equipment and fit-out, (3) similar listings on mybusiness.ge in the same category and city. Without financial figures, a fair price cannot be judged; ask for them before negotiating.

## Revenue vs. profit
Revenue (turnover) is all money coming in; profit is what remains after rent, salaries, suppliers, utilities, taxes and other costs. A café with 30,000 GEL monthly revenue might keep 10–20% as profit in a good case; listings that state only revenue are not telling you the profit.
