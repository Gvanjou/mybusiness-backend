## What due diligence is
Due diligence is the financial, legal and operational check of a business before paying for it. A standard package covers the last 3 years of accounts, verification of tax debts with the Revenue Service of Georgia, whether the lease can be transferred, validity of licences, and the main supplier contracts. According to the mybusiness.ge FAQ it usually costs 1,500–4,000 GEL and takes 2–4 weeks.

## Documents to request from a seller
- Monthly revenue and expenses for at least the last 12 months (ideally 3 years), and bank statements that match them.
- Tax declarations and a tax-debt certificate from the Revenue Service.
- Lease agreement: rent, end date, renewal terms and whether it can be transferred to a new owner.
- Equipment and inventory list with condition and ownership.
- Staff list, salaries, contracts, and who is willing to stay.
- Licences and permits (food safety, alcohol, health, etc.) and whether they transfer.
- Supplier and customer contracts, especially large customers.
- Company extract from the public registry (napr.gov.ge) for share deals: owners, liens, disputes.

## Verifying revenue claims
Seller figures are claims until verified. Compare declared revenue with bank statements and POS/cash-register reports, check that expenses (rent, salaries, utilities, suppliers) are realistic for the stated revenue, and watch at the location at different times. A large gap between "real" and declared revenue is a risk, not a bonus: you cannot prove it and it creates tax exposure.
