## How buying a business in Georgia works
Buying an operating business usually goes through five steps: choosing from the mybusiness.ge catalogue, first contact with the owner or broker, financial and legal due diligence, signing an NDA where sensitive data is shared, and finally a notarised purchase agreement. From first contact to closing typically takes 4–8 weeks (mybusiness.ge FAQ). Prices start around 15,000 GEL for small retail points and go much higher for hotels and production.

## Asset deal vs. share deal
A business can be bought as a share deal (you buy the company, an LLC, with its history, contracts, licences and liabilities) or as an asset deal (you buy equipment, inventory, brand and possibly the lease, and run them through your own company). A share deal keeps contracts and licences but also inherits hidden debts and tax liabilities; an asset deal is cleaner but contracts, the lease and licences may need to be re-signed. Which one is right depends on the business; a lawyer should advise on the final structure.

## Working with brokers and agencies
Many listings on mybusiness.ge are posted by agencies or brokers rather than owners. Brokers can speed things up, but they represent the seller. Ask who pays the broker, request direct access to the owner for key questions, and verify every figure independently.

## Negotiation basics
Asking prices are a starting point. Negotiation is easier when you can point to concrete gaps: missing financial records, a lease ending soon, owner dependence, equipment condition. Agree in writing what is included (equipment list, stock, brand, website, phone numbers, supplier contracts) and consider a transition period where the seller trains you.

## After choosing a business: next steps
1. Contact the seller and request documents (see due diligence).
2. Visit at different times of day and week; count customers.
3. Run due diligence (2–4 weeks).
4. Agree price, structure and what is included; sign an NDA if needed.
5. Sign at a notary; register changes at the Public Service Hall / NAPR.
Use a lawyer for the contract and an accountant for the financial review.
