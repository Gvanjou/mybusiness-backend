## Common risks when buying a small business
- Owner dependence: customers come for the owner personally, or the owner does key work that is not documented.
- Lease risk: short remaining term, no transfer clause, or planned rent increase.
- Unverifiable income: revenue not visible in bank statements or tax declarations.
- Seasonality: tourist businesses can earn most of the year's money in a few months.
- Hidden liabilities in a share deal: tax debts, supplier debts, legal disputes.
- Staff leaving after the sale.
- Competition: a new competitor nearby or a declining street.

## Red flags in a listing
- "Stable income" or "profitable" with no numbers.
- Urgent sale with no clear reason.
- Price far above similar listings without explanation.
- No exact location or photos of the actual premises.
- Seller refuses to show bank statements or tax declarations.
- Equipment described as "everything included" without a list.

## Passive income from a business: what is realistic
Truly passive small businesses are rare. Most cafés, bakeries and shops need the owner present daily, at least in the first year. Businesses become closer to passive when a capable manager is already in place and stays after the sale, which also reduces profit by the manager's salary. Rental property is usually the more passive alternative, with lower but steadier returns.
