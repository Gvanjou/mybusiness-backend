## How to assess competition before buying a business
First decide where the business actually competes. Cafés, bakeries, pharmacies, salons and car washes compete locally (a few hundred metres to a couple of kilometres). Hotels compete within their town or resort. Online stores, producers and wineries compete nationally or by sales channel, so the street address matters much less. Then measure the competitors in that area: how many there are, how busy they are, their prices and their reviews.

## Checking local competition on site
- Walk the area within 300–500 m and count places of the same type; online maps miss many small businesses.
- Visit at peak and quiet hours on weekdays and weekends; count customers at the business and at the two or three nearest competitors.
- Compare prices, menu or product range, opening hours and online reviews (Google Maps, Facebook, booking platforms).
- Ask the seller how sales changed when a nearby competitor opened or closed, and whether anything new is planned in the area.

## Signs of a saturated market
- Many similar businesses within walking distance with few customers.
- Several businesses of the same type for sale in the same city or district at the same time.
- Competitors discounting heavily or closing.
A crowded area is not automatically bad (it can mean strong demand), but the business then needs a clear reason for customers to choose it: location, quality, price, speed or a loyal customer base.
